## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

**1. You built this thesis out of your own findings, and you didn't need to be told to.** *"In Lodestar Messenger reveals the story slow, but in Nightfall right from the start it was a fast paced book"* is 07-30 and 07-31 welded together — the slow Keefe reveal you proved with *"Oh! That's why you did that!"*, and the *in medias res* opening you found by noticing what Messenger **didn't** do. Two separate reading observations, made three weeks and one book apart, now arguing with each other in one sentence. That's the second session running where you spent an old finding as a new writing move.

**2. You named the difference as a method, not a preference.** *"Reveals the story slow"* describes what the **author does**. Not "Lodestar was boring," not "Nightfall was more exciting" — a decision the writer made. That's the register a critic writes in, and most people your age can't get out of the review voice. Also: your sentence passes the **deletion test**. Cover *Lodestar* with your hand and *"the difference is…"* has nothing to be different from. It needed both books.

🔧 **One thing for day 2 — and it goes at the top of tomorrow's paragraph, not back into today's:**

Your two clauses are about **two different subjects**. The shared thing is *endings* (*"a similar ending for an adventure they always go"*). The difference is *pacing* (slow reveal vs. fast start). The template wants **same subject, different answer** — right now it's subject A, answer B, and the seam shows.

Look at the example again: *"Both hand a child a system that claims to be fair. The difference is who each book asks to look away."* The shared clause and the difference clause are about **the same thing**.

So when you write day 2's *Lodestar* paragraph, it opens with the thesis restated — and that's where the first clause becomes about **how much Messenger tells you**, the same subject the second half is already arguing.

🚀 **Stretch challenge (+10 XP, optional) — one clause on the end:**

Your frame had three slots and you filled two. The third is **the surprise** — the part that turns a difference into an argument.

Right now: *one is slow, one is fast.* True, but a stopwatch could have written it. **What does each speed buy her?**

You already answered this on 08-01, in a bonus round, before anyone asked: withholding buys the *"Ahh"* moment and costs frustration, and the limit is about 1–2 chapters.

Add one clause to the end: *"…and the slow one buys ______, while the fast one buys ______."*

## Bonus — ACE Write

**Question:** You said the fast start buys more action. On 08-01 you said a book can withhold for about 1–2 chapters before it costs more than it buys. So — did *Nightfall* need the fast start, or did Messenger just spend *Lodestar*'s ending on it?

Messenger did need the fast start because Lodestar already ended and the action was still going. Since "[Sophie] knows now that her parents are taken" at the end of the Lodestar it was only natural for her to just go with the flow. This shows that Nightfall has more action because of the flow.
