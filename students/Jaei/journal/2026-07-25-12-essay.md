## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

1. **Your thesis argues with the obvious answer.** *"The 39 Clues is **not** about the bloodthirsty battle between the family to win, it's about the ending…"* — ten books of Kabras and Holts betraying each other, and your claim is that the fight was never the point. That's a claim a reader could disagree with, which is the whole test of a thesis. "Not X, it's Y" is the same shape the *Holes* example used.
2. **You spotted the shape of the entire series in one clause:** *"their enemy, who wasn't Cahill but was in the 39 clue hunt for over a century."* The real antagonist was outside the family the whole time, hidden inside a family feud. That's not plot recall — that's structure, and it's what makes your thesis defensible instead of just an opinion.

✅ **Watch list — clean, first draft.** Two compound subjects in one paragraph (*"Amy and Dan earned"*, *"Amy and Dan full fill"*) and both correct, fifteen minutes after learning the rule. Plus you heard *"ingredient"* → *"ingredients"* on your own read-aloud. Clean streak: **1/3**.

🔧 **One thing to level up (make the quote do work):** Right now the quote is dropped in rather than proving anything — *"it shows that Amy and Dan 'full fill the dearest wish of Olivia Cahill.'"* A reader who hasn't read the book has no idea what Olivia's wish **was**, so the quote can't prove your point yet. You already own this move (07-23, the POV quote). Try:

> *Amy and Dan finally "full fill the dearest wish of Olivia Cahill" — the wish that ______ — which proves ______.*

🚀 **Stretch challenge (+10 XP, optional):** Your focus skill is **letting characters talk**. Somewhere at the end of *Into the Gauntlet*, when the branches agree to trust each other, somebody *says* something out loud. Find that actual line in the book and put it in your paragraph in quotation marks. Reported speech tells us it happened; a spoken line lets us hear it.
