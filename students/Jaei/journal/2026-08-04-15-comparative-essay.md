**2. You used the quote's POSITION as the evidence, not its words.**
*"Let's go to the ravagog"* doesn't sound slow. Nothing about those five words proves anything
about pacing. What proves it is **"in the middle part of the book"** — the thing the whole story
is aimed at doesn't arrive until you're halfway through it.

That's citing **when**, and it's the second session running you've done it — on day 1 you proved
*Nightfall*'s opening with a line from *"the end of the Lodestar."* Marking where evidence sits
in a book is a scholar's move, and most people never think to do it at all.

🔧 **One thing — and it goes into day 3's paragraph, not back into tonight's:**

Your quote is in the room, but you never tell the reader what its job is. The sentence says
*"this clearly shows that it is a slow revealing book"* — which repeats the claim instead of
connecting it to the proof. The connection is **"in the middle part of the book,"** and right
now that phrase is sitting in the sentence as a detail rather than as the argument.

A quote becomes evidence when one clause says *why **that** line, in **that** place, means what
you say it means.*

So when you write day 3's *Nightfall* paragraph, its quote needs that clause built in from the
start — not *"this shows the book is fast,"* but the sentence that says why a line landing on
**page one** proves it.
