## Tutor feedback

🌟 **You used your own craft finding as a writing tool — three weeks later.**
On 2026-07-16 you read *Neverseen* and noticed that **"You kids"** is Mr. Forkle's
signature opener — you wrote that the author could have said "Welcome" and chose
the gruff line instead, to keep his cover. Today you had to *write* Forkle's
dialogue, and unprompted you reached for **"You kids sure do shout a lot."** That
is not remembering a detail. That is taking something you noticed as a reader and
spending it as a writer. Most people never close that loop at all.

🌟 **Your ear caught the verb, and the item stayed clean.**
First draft was *"You kids sure **does** shout a lot."* You read it out loud and it
came back **"do"** — no flag from me. That's the plural-subject shape that took
seven corrections in June, killed by one read-aloud pass. And 👉 **word in front**
came back clean across four chains (*were preparing · do shout · gotta work ·
let's see*) → Watch streak **0/3 → 1/3**. You also added *"Real elfy Sophie thought"*
in the revision — that's her thought sitting in the narration without a "she
thought that," which is a real technique and you did it by ear.

🔧 **One thing to level up: the comma that lives inside the quotation marks.**
When a spoken line is followed by *who said it*, the line ends with a **comma**,
not nothing — and the comma goes **inside** the closing mark. Your sentence:

> "I thought you were dead. I was so worried" Sophie said.
> → "I thought you were dead. I was so worried**,**" Sophie said.

Same for *"You kids sure do shout a lot**,**" Mr. Forkle chuckled.* The rule once:
**a spoken line handing off to a speaker tag ends in a comma; a spoken line that
ends the sentence ends in a period.** Both live inside the quotation marks.

🚀 **Stretch challenge (+10 XP, optional): the ripple.**
Tier 3 wants one consequence the character doesn't see. Right now your scene ends
on Forkle's line, and everything in it is something Sophie is looking straight at.
Add **one sentence at the very end** about something in that room that Sophie does
not notice — the reader sees it, she doesn't. In *The Giver* example it was the
camera rotating toward the sound of their voices while the two boys argued.

**What the stretch added, and why it works:**

- **"a flick of movement"** — not a person, not a shadow. A *flick* is the smallest
  unit of noticing, which is exactly why nobody in the room catches it.
- **The misdirection was already built.** The scene opens with all three of them
  staring at a moonlark nest through a gigantic window — the brightest thing in the
  room. The danger is in the darkest corner behind them. The setup was written before
  the payoff existed.
- **The Neverseen is named last** — cloth, then symbol, then the name. The reader
  assembles it in the order Sophie would have, if she had turned around.

**Two repairs made during the stretch round:**

- *"Both hands with tight grip…"* was a fragment standing on its own; he joined it to
  the previous sentence with a comma. (Not what he'd been pointed at — he found it on
  the way.)
- *"All of them **was** not aware"* → *"All of them **were** not aware."* Fixed on the
  two-word cue ✌️ **"two fingers"** — no rule restated, no eighth correction.
