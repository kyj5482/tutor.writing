## My writing

The 39 Clues is not about the bloodthirsty battle between the family to win, it's about the ending when Amy and Dan earned the trust from everyone and got all the ingredients to the master serum. This part was most important because it shows that Amy and Dan "full fill the dearest wish of Olivia Cahill." The trust unites the Cahill family again to fight against their enemy, who wasn't Cahill but was in the 39 clue hunt for over a century.

What really shows this is the trust in this bloody and backstabbing hunt. This was designed so that people would betray each other until the end. The ending was just as what Grace planned. Alistair, who had betrayed them before, handed Dan his clues and said, "I trust you and Amy" and one by one the other branches did the same. That is the proof of how the trust between the branches was built.

Some might argue that during the 10 books Cahill family betrayed too much for a one ending to cover it all, and it's a fair point. Since they did betray each others a lot during the whole series. But, what happened at the end decided the fate of the Cahill family: the battle against another brutal family and that's the most important part. No matter how much they betrayed each other the fact that they are trusting each other is the most crucial part.

## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

1. **You made the quote mean something by naming who says it.** The line alone is ordinary — anybody can say *"I trust you and Amy."* What you wrote was *"Alistair, **who had betrayed them before**, handed Dan his clues and said, 'I trust you and Amy'"*. That clause is the argument. It turns a sentence of dialogue into evidence that trust was *built*, because we watch it get built by the person least likely to give it. Three days ago this paragraph ended on *"This is the proof"* with no proof in it. Now the proof is the sentence.
2. **Your concession genuinely costs you something, and you still win the point.** *"Some might argue that during the 10 books Cahill family betrayed too much for a one ending to cover it all, and it's a fair point. Since they did betray each others a lot."* You didn't shrink the objection to make it easy — you agreed with it flat out. Then: *"No matter how much they betrayed each other the fact that they are trusting each other is the most crucial part."* That's not "the ending matters more," that's **the betrayal doesn't get erased, it gets outweighed.** Different claim, harder claim, and it's the one that answers the objection.
