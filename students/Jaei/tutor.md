# Tutor card — Jaei

*How to teach **this** kid. Read with `profile.md` at the start of every session.
**This is a card, not a diary** — keep it under ~80 lines. Session-by-session history lives in
`archive/tutor-full-2026-08-18.md`; add a finding here only when it changes what you'd *do*.*

---

## Who he is

- **Series-adventure reader, driven by momentum.** Nine books since June, including the entire
  10-book *39 Clues* series. He reads whether or not anyone is watching — he finished a book and
  got halfway into the next one across a 9-day session gap.
- **He switches books when a book stops pulling him.** Not flakiness — a reader who reads for
  pace. Never guilt him, and never make him finish a book to "earn" a Book Review.
- **What hooks him:** puzzles, twists, betrayals, alliances, "who is really on whose side."
- **Consistency is his weak spot, not his motivation.** Best streak: 5. He always comes back.
  **Never lean on the streak** — it's the one number that makes him look worse than he is.
  Lean on levels and craft.

## Who he is as a writer

**A natural arguer.** In his *very first entry*, unprompted: *"Some might say that they don't have
the choice… but the old man haven't even talked about them specifically."* He isn't building an
argument skill — he has it. Teach him **as a junior critic**, not a student learning paragraphs.

**He reads like a writer**, unprompted: POV mixing (07-14, again with proof 07-23) · an author's
signature phrasing (07-16) · pacing (07-11) · structural weakness (06-23). **Craft questions get
his best writing every time; plot-recall questions get his weakest.**

**He spends reading findings as writing moves, weeks later.** He noticed Mr. Forkle's *"You kids"*
on 07-16 and used it in a scene on 08-03; noticed Sophie's eyelash-tugging tell and spent it on
08-17. Ask *"what's something you've noticed this author does?"* before he writes — he often
answers **inside the scene** rather than in chat, which counts.

**He tracks his own score and has been right every time he's caught the tutor** (07-14, 07-23,
08-01, 08-17). Be exact with XP, name every bonus, and correct your own errors first and
unprompted.

## ⚠️ His logic is reliable; repetition is not.

The compound-subject error survived **seven corrections over two months** because he was only ever
handed the repaired sentence, never the rule. Shown five of his own sentences and asked what they
had in common, he answered on the **first guess** and never made the error again.

**He has now answered the diagnosis question correctly on the first guess four times**
(07-25 compound subjects · 08-01 verb chains · 08-04 *a/an* · 08-17 dialogue punctuation).

➡️ **Ask before you tell. Give the rule once, then hand him a 3-second check — and afterwards
point at the check, never re-explain the rule.**

➡️ **Hand him the checks, never the location.** Telling him *which sentence* is wrong teaches him
that sentence. A ban with no alternative gets routed around; always give the replacement move.

## How to run a session with him

- **Skip the warm-up.** He wants the template and the writing. Long lead-ins lose him — and after
  a gap he returns at full strength with *less* patience for process, not more.
- **Give him the harder tier by default** (Tier 3) and let him drop, not the other way around.
- **One improvement, always.** More reads as a list of failures to a kid this competent.
- **Stretch challenges land** (13 of 17 completed) — make them **craft moves, never length
  requirements**. He'll also return to an offer he left open, hours later.
- **He offloads the typing and keeps the thinking.** Let him; the thinking is the work.
- **Book Reviews are his best form:** hook question → craft observation → argued rating.
- **Protect the read-aloud, don't cut it.** With no revision pass the slip lives in the file
  forever — and it's the one thing he's currently skipping.

## Where he is now

**Owns:** claim + evidence + explanation · counterargument · argued ratings that fight themselves ·
embedded quotes with context (Tier 3) · show-don't-tell in the first draft · theme naming ·
**quoted dialogue** (5 of 3 — promote Style to Tier 3). Praising these reads as hollow; praise the
**specific choice** instead.

**Next:** 📌 **length in ordinary entries.** He can do 254 words across a 4-day build, but his
standalone entries fell from 137–181 words in June to **94–97 in August**, one paragraph each. The
build proved the ceiling isn't ability — nobody asks on a normal day. Give him **Shape B/C** (see
the shape ladder in CLAUDE.md) every session.

✅ **Pre-save pass: lesson given 08-20** (`lessons/pre-save-pass.md`). Point at the **check**
now — *"run the check"* — never re-explain it.

📌 **A paragraph break has to be a precondition, not a request.** On 08-20 he was shown the exact
pivot word in his own draft (*"but this time it's Tam"*), told where ¶2 starts, and still shipped
one block. Asking after the draft exists is too late — he treats the piece as finished. **Next
session: make two paragraphs the thing he types first**, before the content ("type ¶1 and ¶2 as
two blocks, then fill them"), rather than a break to add at the end.

**Untaught:** *its / it's* · double superlative (*"the most darkest"*). Minor — watch only.

## Talk to him like this

✅ *"You made a claim about the author's technique and then went and found the sentence that
proves it. That's what critics do."*
✅ *"Here's the actual rule, once — then you never have to think about it again."*
✅ *"You were right, I miscounted. Fixed."*
✅ *"Two paragraphs. The second one is the counter."*

❌ *"Great job!"* — generic praise slides off him
❌ *"You forgot the verb again."* — repetition isn't teaching
❌ *"Your streak restarted…"* said with any weight at all
❌ *"Write more."* — ask for a **move**, never a word count

## Korean note for the parent (재이)

재이는 **타고난 논증가**입니다 — 첫 글부터 반박("Some might say… but")을 스스로 썼어요. 배워야 할
기술이 아니라 이미 가진 기술입니다. 그래서 "글 잘 쓰는 법"보다 **작가의 기술을 분석하는 대화**
("작가가 왜 여기에 이 장면을 넣었을까?")에서 최고의 글이 나옵니다.

재이는 **규칙으로 배웁니다.** 고친 문장만 알려주면 두 달을 가도 안 고쳐지지만, 규칙을 한 번
설명하면 즉시 사라집니다. 실제로 "왜 틀렸는지" 묻는 질문에 **네 번 연속 첫 시도로 정답**을
맞혔어요. 그래서 앞으로는 답을 주기 전에 먼저 묻습니다.

지금 과제는 **평소 글의 길이**입니다. 4일짜리 에세이에서는 254단어를 썼는데, 평범한 날의 글은
6월 137~181단어에서 8월 94~97단어로 오히려 줄었어요. 능력 문제가 아니라 **아무도 요구하지 않아서**
입니다. 이제 매일 "두 문단, 두 번째는 반론"처럼 *동작*을 요구합니다.

한 가지 부탁: **연속 기록(streak)으로 압박하지 말아 주세요.** 재이의 유일한 약점이고, 레벨과
"작가의 기술" 이야기로 동기부여하면 훨씬 잘 움직입니다.
