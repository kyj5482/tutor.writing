# Tutor card — Jaei

*How to teach **this** kid. Built 2026-07-25 from all 17 journal entries (2026-06-19 → 2026-07-25).
Read this together with `profile.md` at the start of every session. Update it at each weekly.*

## Who he is as a reader

- **Series-adventure reader, driven by momentum.** Six books finished in five weeks:
  *The Scorch Trials* → *Everblaze* → four straight *39 Clues* → *Storm Warning*.
- **He switches books when a book stops pulling him** (*Neverseen* set aside twice, 07-16
  and 07-25). This is not flakiness — it's a reader who reads for pace. Don't guilt him
  about it, and don't make him finish a book to "earn" a Book Review.
- **What hooks him:** puzzles, twists, betrayals, alliances, high stakes, "who is really
  on whose side."
- **Consistency is his weak spot, not his motivation.** Best streak is 5; he resets often
  (07-09, 07-12, 07-17→20, 07-22, 07-24). He always comes back. **Never lean on the
  streak to motivate him** — it's the one number that makes him look worse than he is.
  Lean on levels and craft instead.

## Who he is as a writer

**He is a natural arguer.** This is the headline. In his *very first entry* (06-19), with
no prompting, he wrote:

> *"Some might say that they don't have the choice because they need the cure for the Flare,
> but the old man haven't even talked about them specifically…"*

An 8th grader who reaches for a counterargument unprompted on day one is not building
an argument skill — he already has it. Teach him **as a junior critic**, not as a
student learning to write paragraphs.

**He reads like a writer.** Unprompted, across the corpus, he has noticed:
- POV craft, twice — *"mixing the third and first person point of view"* (07-14), then
  again with a quote as proof (07-23)
- an author's signature phrasing — *"'You kids' … it's Mr. Forkle's favorite word to start with"* (07-16)
- pacing — *"the pace and the twists whenever the book feels peaceful"* (07-11)
- structural weakness — *"the ending was really bad because it was basically reaping itself
  from the begin[n]ing"* (06-23)

**Feed this.** Craft questions ("why did the author put this here instead of there?") get
his best writing every single time. Plot-recall questions get his weakest.

**He tracks his own score.** He has caught the tutor's bookkeeping twice — an undercounted
Quote Catcher (07-14) and a dropped stretch bonus (07-23). **Both times he was right.**
Be exact with his XP, name every bonus out loud, and if you make a mistake, say so plainly
and fix it. He respects accuracy and he is watching.

## What he already owns (don't spend praise here)

Claim + evidence + explanation · counterargument · argued ratings that fight themselves ·
embedded quotes with context (Tier 3) · show-don't-tell in the first draft · theme naming.

Praising these as if they're new reads as hollow to him. Praise the **specific choice**
instead: *"You quoted the POV line as proof instead of just saying you noticed it — that's
the move."*

## ✅ 2026-07-25 — the lesson landed, and the read-aloud mystery is solved

Three things learned in one session, all worth keeping:

1. **The rule worked in under fifteen minutes.** Shown the five sentences and asked what they
   had in common, he answered on the **first guess**: *"the verb matches the word right before
   it."* He then wrote two compound subjects in his next paragraph and got both right with no
   flag from me. Six weeks of handing him the fix did nothing; one rule did it immediately.
   **This is the strongest evidence in the file that he learns by rule, not by correction.**
   Every future mechanical error should go straight to a lesson, not to a seventh reminder.
2. **He never knew the mic button existed.** He asked *"how do i read it to you"*. The
   read-aloud has been marked skipped for nineteen entries and it was read as reluctance — it
   was a **missing instruction**. Never assume he's declining a step; check that he knows how
   to do it. (Told him the plainer version too: read it out loud to the room and type "read
   it" — the mouth and ears are the point, the button is a convenience. He did it and
   self-caught a slip.)
3. **He chose the ladder over the points.** Offered 12 Essay (35 XP today) or 10 Book Review
   (40 XP, plus his best form), he picked the essay. Targets beat rewards with this kid — show
   him the milestone and he'll take the harder road to it. Corollary: don't dangle XP to steer
   him, dangle the *stage*.

## ⚠️ The diagnosis that matters — why the verb error won't die

He has been corrected on compound subjects **seven times in two months** and the streak is
still 0/3. That is not carelessness. Look at what he actually writes:

| What he wrote | The verb agrees with… |
|---|---|
| *"Teresa and Aris **was**"* (06-23) | Aris |
| *"me and my sister **was**"* (07-07) | my sister |
| *"trust and loyalty **is**"* (07-14) | loyalty |
| *"Sophie and her friends **knows**"* (07-16) | *(singular default)* |
| *"the plot and the action **is**"* (07-23) | the action |
| *"Amy and Dan **was**"* (07-25) | Dan |

**He is matching the verb to the nearest noun, not to the whole subject.** Every single
time. This is a real, nameable rule gap — it even has a name in grammar ("proximity
agreement") — and *nobody has ever explained the rule to him.* He has only ever been
handed the corrected sentence.

**So stop correcting it and teach it once.** → `lessons/compound-subjects.md`

He is an analyst. Give him the *why* and he will apply it; give him the fix and he will
nod and repeat the error next week. Six weeks of evidence says so.

## How to run a session with him

- **Skip the warm-up.** He wants the template and the writing. Long lead-ins lose him.
- **Give him the harder tier.** He has written Tier 3 on his last four entries. Offer
  Tier 3 by default and let him drop to Tier 2, not the other way around.
- **One improvement, always.** He can absorb more, but the rule holds — more than one
  reads as a list of failures to a kid this competent.
- **Stretch challenges land** — he's completed 13 of 17. Make them craft moves, not
  length requirements.
- **Short days:** he stops after the main entry (07-25). That's allowed. But the read-aloud
  is the *first* thing to protect, not the first thing to cut — because with no revision
  pass, the slip stays in the file forever.
- **Book Reviews are his best form.** Hook question → craft observation → argued rating.
  He's written five and each one is stronger than the last.

## Talk to him like this

✅ *"You made a claim about the author's technique and then went and found the sentence
that proves it. That's what critics do."*
✅ *"Here's the actual rule, once — then you never have to think about it again."*
✅ *"You were right, I miscounted. Fixed."*

❌ *"Great job!"* (generic praise slides off him)
❌ *"You forgot the verb again."* (seventh time — repetition isn't teaching)
❌ *"Your streak restarted…"* said with any weight at all

## Korean note for the parent (재이)

재이는 **타고난 논증가**입니다 — 첫 글(06-19)부터 반박("Some might say… but")을 스스로 썼어요.
이건 배워야 할 기술이 아니라 이미 가진 기술입니다. 그래서 "글 잘 쓰는 법"보다 **작가의 기술을
분석하는 대화**("작가가 왜 여기에 이 장면을 넣었을까?")에서 최고의 글이 나옵니다.

동사 실수가 두 달째 반복되는 이유를 찾았습니다. 재이는 **동사를 '바로 앞 명사'에 맞추고 있습니다**
("Amy and Dan **was**" → Dan에 맞춤). 규칙을 몰라서지 부주의해서가 아니에요. 지금까지는 고친 문장만
알려줬지 **규칙 자체를 설명한 적이 없습니다.** 이제 짧은 수업으로 한 번 제대로 가르칩니다.

한 가지 부탁: **연속 기록(streak)으로 압박하지 말아 주세요.** 재이의 유일한 약점이고, 대신 레벨과
"작가의 기술" 이야기로 동기부여하면 훨씬 잘 움직입니다.
