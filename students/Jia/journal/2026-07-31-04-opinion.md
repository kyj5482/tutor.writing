## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well**

1. **"...even though he knows that Dumbledore is not alive."** This is the best sentence in the paragraph and it's doing something genuinely hard. Harry is keeping a promise to someone who can never check on him, never thank him, never know. That is the difference between **obeying** an instruction and being **loyal** to a person — and she found it in one clause, unprompted. Nobody asked her what kind of loyalty it was.
2. **All four parts, and the explanation stands on its own** — claim → evidence → *"This shows that…"* → wrap-up. The explanation sentence is the one most kids skip, and she has now written it every session for weeks. Also: *"the adventure he **and** Dumbledore **went** on"* — compound subject, plural verb, correct and unprompted for the **fourth** session running. The retired item stays retired.

🔧 **One thing to level up: your wrap-up changed the subject.**

Look at the two ends of the paragraph side by side:

> **Claim:** *"I think it **was right** for Harry to not tell anyone…"*
> **Wrap-up:** *"That's why I think Harry is **very responsible**."*

Those are two different claims. She started arguing about whether a *choice* was right and finished describing what kind of *person* Harry is. The Tier-2 wrap-up's job is to say **the same claim** in different words, so the paragraph closes where it opened.

The frame she was given to try:

> *"That's why I think Harry was right to ______."*
