## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

**1. You picked the person who isn't the hero — and you went straight to what it costs her.**

The chapter is full of Harry. You wrote about **McGonagall**, and the thing you chose to say about
her is that she *"would sacrifice her life for Harry's mission."* Not that she's powerful, not that
she's clever — what it **costs** her.

That is the same instinct as Sirius getting quieter (07-05), Slughorn's guilt (07-28), Lupin afraid
of what he passed to his child (08-01), and Harry worrying about other people's lives (07-31).
**Five different books-worth of chapters, one instinct.** It's your subject, and it's the reason
your writing is worth reading.

**2. Your counterargument doesn't just show the other side — it concedes it and beats it anyway.**

> *"Some people might say that it was because of the mission Harry was on was ordered by Dumbledore,
> **but even if it was why, she still would have helped** Harry hold off Voldemort."*

Read what that actually does. You let the other side be **true** — maybe she really was following
Dumbledore's orders — and then you argue that it *doesn't change the answer.* That's a different
and much harder move than "some people might say X, but they're wrong."

That move has a name and a ladder stage: it's ⚖️ **Debater**, stage 5. On **07-14** you needed a
frame to write a counterargument at all. Tonight it's just how you finish a paragraph.

🔧 **One thing — and it's for the *next* one, not this one:**

Your *"This shows"* sentence says: *"This shows professor McGonagall will sacrifice her life for
Harry's mission."* Look at your opening: *"The bravest person in this part isn't Harry. It is
professor McGonagall."*

Those are the same idea. The sentence explains the claim by repeating it.

Compare what the Percy Jackson example did with its *This shows*: *"some secrets in this book
**aren't lies, they're shields.**"* That's a brand-new thought — something the claim never said.

Here is the whole fix, as a shape:

> This shows ______. **It also means ______.**

The first blank is what you already wrote. The second one is where the new idea goes.
