## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

**1. You changed your line — and that's the whole session right there.**

She had *"tell the truth"* — a good line, and she'd already spotted something real about it (a stranger says it to Mark, *"she doesn't know him"*). Then she went back into the book and came out with **"We'll walk until we die?"** Nobody asked her to. **That's a writer being unsatisfied with her own material**, and it is the first time in 27 entries she has done it. She traded a safe line for a harder one.

**2. 🎯 "I think he *might* have said that because…"**

Not *"he said it because he knew he would die."* She wrote **might**. She offered a reading and left room to be wrong — a reader making an argument instead of announcing a fact. Nobody taught her that hedge; it arrived on its own.

*(Also worth recording: both `"` marks, opened and closed, unprompted — eleven days after that Watch item retired. And her subject is the same one it has been for eight entries running: not what happens, but **what it costs the person it happens to.**)*

🔧 **One thing to level up — for the next Golden Line, not tonight:**

**Put us in the room.** The paragraph says what the line *means* but never where we're standing.

> **She wrote:** *"I was very curious why Mark had said that."*
> **One sentence earlier:** *"Mark says this on the mountain, in the snow, to a dog who can't answer him."*

Eight seconds of writing, and the line goes from interesting to painful. Tier 2 Golden Line asks for it by name: *what is happening in the story at this moment.* Next time — **scene first, then the meaning.**
