## Tutor feedback

🌟 **Two things you did well:**

1. **You gave Lupin a secret and then had him refuse to say it — in the diary where he's supposed to be honest.** *"When Harry saw me, I didn't tell him why I was really here for."* That is a **diary entry about not telling the truth**, which is a genuinely sophisticated thing to build. The reader now knows two things at once: what Lupin came for, and that he couldn't say it out loud. Nobody asked you for that. It's the same move as Slughorn's guilt on 07-28 — you go looking for the thing the character is hiding, and you find it every time.

2. **Your last line is the scariest sentence in the entry, and it's disguised as a hope.** *"But I hope that baby is not going to be the warewolf."* The template asked for a hope. You wrote a hope that is really a **dread** — a father hoping his child didn't inherit the thing he hates about himself. That is the actual center of Lupin's whole story in this book, and you got to it in a sentence, in his voice, while he's standing in a hallway not telling anyone why he came. **You keep going straight to what a thing costs somebody.** That's your subject.

🔧 **One thing to level up — put the feeling in his body.**

Look at Brian's diary next to yours:

> **Brian:** *"My heart was slamming so hard I could hear it over the engine."*
> **Yours:** *"I'm worried that Harry would be mad at me."*

Yours **names** the feeling. Brian's **shows** it — heart, engine, the sound of it. You do this every time you write a diary (07-28 was the same: *"I was a part of the reason why everyone is scared"* — a beautiful sentence that lives entirely in his head, never in his hands).

You have a perfect spot for it already: *"When Harry saw me, I didn't tell him."* What was Lupin's **body** doing while he didn't tell him? Hands, throat, eyes, the door he was still holding?

🚀 **Stretch challenge (+10 XP, optional): one new sentence, no rewriting.**

Add **one** sentence to the diary — right after Harry speaks. Fill this in:

> *When Harry said that, my ____________.*

One body part. One thing it did. That's the whole stretch — and it's a brand-new sentence, not a repair.

## Bonus — ACE Write
