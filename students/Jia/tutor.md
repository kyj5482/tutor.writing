## Who she is

- **Immersive, one-world-deep reader with real stamina.** Harry Potter books 3→7 (~3,400 pages)
  between 06-09 and 08-06, including the 766-page *Order of the Phoenix*. Not a book-hopper —
  she commits to a world and stays. Her limit is time, not attention.
- **What hooks her:** friendship and loyalty, unfairness done to someone she likes, secrets
  and backstory.
- **She is a noticer.** She catches small human things other readers skim: *"Sirius Black was
  first really happy for him but became moodier and surlier… and talk less to everyone."* (07-05)
  Nobody asked her about Sirius's mood.
- **Her subject is what a choice COSTS someone.** Sirius going quiet · Slughorn's guilt · Lupin
  afraid of what he passed to his child · McGonagall trading her life. Six entries, one instinct,
  never prompted. It's why her writing is worth reading.
- **She is honest and a little funny** — *"One thing that could be better is if Professor Umbridge
  died in the end."* Never sand this off.

## ⚠️ Her ear is excellent. Her eye is not.

The single most useful fact about teaching her. Every time she reads aloud she catches her own
errors (*ask→asked*, *show→shows*, *Hermonian→Hermione*, *Harry like→Harry likes*) — **six
consecutive sessions where her ear beat her eye.** Silent proofreading catches her nothing.

**But match the tool to the error:**

| Error type | Tool | Why |
|-----------|------|-----|
| Endings (-ed / -s), tense, word choice | 🔊 **read aloud** | She hears it instantly |
| Agreement with *and* (*"Neville and Luna says"*) | 📖 **the rule** | It *sounds* fine — the ear will never catch it |
| Quotation marks, possessive `'s` | ✌️ **a count** | Marks are silent; you can't hear punctuation |

## How to run a session with her

- **Warm-up works on her** (unlike Jaei). Greeting, streak, badge count — keep the game visible.
- **Concrete gets done; abstract gets declined.** "Add a counterargument" ✅, "zoom into one word"
  ✅ — but an open *"add a 3-sentence So what?"* was declined repeatedly. **Write every stretch as
  a fill-in-the-blank with an example sentence.**
- **Show both slots on screen.** Her one-sentence explanation was flagged three times and never
  moved. Printing the shape — *"This shows ______. It also means ______."* — fixed it in one night.
- **Menus of choices get picked; menus of tasks get skipped.** She chose 🎓 Scholar — the hardest
  stage on the list — from a pick-one menu with sample sentences. A recommendation stated as a
  sentence has bounced every time.
- **Two guesses maximum**, then hand her the answer in one line and move on.
- **Any request from her is engagement, not resistance** — answer it immediately, in the same
  message (she asked for a different ACE question on 08-05 and it went well).
- **Her templates decide what is even measurable.** She produced no quotation marks for six
  straight pieces because nothing in the frame required one. If you want to score a habit, the
  frame has to demand it.
- **Never score a mishearing as her error.** On books full of invented names, budget one confirm
  message per read-aloud. Punishing the habit would end the habit.

## Where she is now

**Owns:** structure & transitions · own-words explanation with *because* · specific names ·
honest opinions · ACE format · a quote inside a main entry (07-27) · paragraph breaks when asked
(07-30) · the two-sentence explanation (08-03, 08-06) · conceding a counterargument (08-05).

**Next:** 📌 **length.** 27 of 29 entries are one paragraph; her longest ever is **162 words** and
🏛️ Essay Writer wants 200+. This is now the only thing standing between her and her chosen goal.
Give her **Shape B** (see the shape ladder in CLAUDE.md) every session until two paragraphs are
automatic, then the 3-day essay build.

**Untaught, not yet raised:** *a / an* before a vowel (*"a unbreakable vow"*).

## Talk to her like this

✅ *"Read that sentence out loud — you'll hear it."* (name her superpower)
✅ *"You noticed which NAME he uses. That's what real readers do."*
✅ *"Try this exact sentence: In the book, ______ said, '______.'"*
✅ *"New paragraph. Start it with **But**."*

❌ *"Check your verbs."* → say **read aloud** instead
❌ *"Add a few sentences about why it mattered."* → too open, she'll decline
❌ *"Write more."* → ask for a **move**, never a word count
❌ Correcting her honesty or her jokes into something more polite

## Korean note for the parent (지아)

지아는 **관찰자형 작가**입니다. 아무도 묻지 않은 작은 것을 스스로 포착해요 — 특히 **"어떤 선택이
그 사람에게 무엇을 치르게 하는가"**를 계속 씁니다. 여섯 편 연속으로, 시킨 적 없이요. 이게 지아
글의 진짜 강점입니다.

가장 중요한 발견: **지아는 '눈'이 아니라 '귀'로 글을 고칩니다.** 소리 내어 읽으면 자기 실수를 거의
다 잡아냅니다(여섯 세션 연속). 다만 *"두 사람인데 is를 쓰는"* 실수와 따옴표·`'s` 같은 건 소리로
안 잡히니 **규칙**과 **세어보기**로 따로 잡습니다.

지금 유일한 과제는 **길이**입니다. 두 달 동안 60~100단어, 거의 전부 한 문단이었어요. 그래서 앞으로는
"더 써라"가 아니라 **"두 번째 문단은 But으로 시작해"** 처럼 *동작 하나*를 더 요구합니다. 길이는
따라옵니다. 집에서는 **"저장하기 전에 소리 내어 한 번 읽었어?"** 한 마디면 충분합니다.
