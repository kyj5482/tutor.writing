# Tutor card — Jia

*How to teach **this** kid. Built 2026-07-25 from all 18 journal entries (2026-06-09 → 2026-07-24).
Read this together with `profile.md` at the start of every session. Update it at each weekly.*

---

## Who she is as a reader

- **Immersive, one-world-deep reader with serious stamina.** Four Harry Potter books
  back-to-back, including *Order of the Phoenix* — **766 pages** — and straight into
  *Half-Blood Prince* the same day.
- **She commits to a world and stays.** *The Land of Stories* (06-09), then Harry Potter
  from book 3 to book 6 without a break. She is not a book-hopper; she goes deep.
- **300-page and 140-page reading days happen.** Her limit is not attention — it's time.
- **What hooks her:** friendship and loyalty, unfairness done to a character she likes
  (Umbridge, being left out, being disbelieved), secrets and backstory.

## Who she is as a writer

**She is a noticer.** This is the headline. She catches small human things other readers
skim past, and she has been doing it since early on:

> *"Harry noticed that Sirius Black was first really happy for him but became moodier and
> surlier than before, and talk less to everyone."* (07-05)

Nobody asked her about Sirius's mood. She saw it.

**Her growth curve is steep.** Compare her first entry to her most recent:

> **06-09:** *"Their grandmother bring a lot of presents for them…"* — four frame sentences
> **07-24:** *"'Dark Lord' I think it means that Snape is Death Eater because no one else
> says Dark Lord they all say you know who or he who must not be named…"*

That second one is **literary analysis** — she noticed that *which name a character uses*
reveals whose side he's on. Six weeks apart. This is a kid moving fast.

**She is honest and a little funny**, and it's genuinely her voice:
> *"One thing that could be better is if Professor Umbridge died in the end."* (07-20)

Never sand this off. Honesty is why her writing is worth reading.

## ⚠️ Her ear is excellent. Her eye is not.

This is the single most useful fact about teaching Jia, and it changes the whole method.

**Evidence:** every time she has been asked to read her work aloud, she has caught her own
errors — *"ask"→"asked"* and *"show"→"shows"* on 07-23, *"THis"→"This"* on 07-15,
*Hermonian→Hermione* on 07-15, *Nevile→Neville* and *becasue→because* on 07-16. She just
cleared an entire Watch item (spelling, 3/3) using nothing but the read-aloud habit.

**But silent proofreading catches her nothing.** The errors that survive are the ones that
*look* fine on the page.

➡️ **So coach her through sound, never through sight.** "Read it out loud" is not a generic
tip for Jia — it is her actual working method, and it is provably the thing that clears her
Watch items. Build every check around hearing it.

Two different things are hiding in one Watch item, and they need different fixes:

1. **Endings (-ed / -s)** — this is a *sound* problem, and her ear fixes it instantly.
   Read-aloud is the whole cure.
2. **Matching the doer** (07-24) — this is a *rule* problem, and reading aloud will **not**
   catch it, because *"Snape and Malfoy is"* sounds fine to a kid who hasn't been taught
   the rule. Note that Jaei makes the identical error, and for the identical reason:
   she matched the verb to **Malfoy**, the nearest noun, instead of to both people.

➡️ Endings: keep using the ear. Matching: **teach the rule once** →
`lessons/compound-subjects.md` (shared with Jaei — they need the same lesson).

## How to run a session with her

- **Warm-up works on her** (unlike Jaei). She responds to the greeting, the streak, the
  badge count. Keep the game visible — it genuinely motivates her.
- **Concrete stretches get done; abstract stretches get declined.** Look at the pattern:
  "add a counterargument" ✅ (07-14), "zoom into one word" ✅ (07-22), but the open-ended
  *"add a 3-sentence So what?"* was declined twice (07-23, 07-24). **Write her stretch as a
  fill-in-the-blank with an example sentence**, and she does it nearly every time.
- **Give her the sentence frame, then get out of the way.** Her Tier-2 structure is solid;
  what she needs is the shape of the *new* move, not encouragement.
- **She has never used paragraph breaks.** When the verb item clears, this is the next
  thing — and it's a one-minute fix that will make her writing look twice as strong.
- **Don't rush her off Harry Potter.** She has two books left in the series. Recommend
  read-alongside books (see `library/reading-ladder.md`) rather than replacements.

## What 07-27 taught us (the mini-lesson session)

**1. Teach a new rule with its OFF switch, or she will over-apply it.** Ten minutes after
learning "and joins two doers → plural verb," she ran the check on a paragraph with no
compound subject at all and offered to change *was→were, is→are*. She had learned "this rule
means change is to are" instead of "this rule means **look for the word *and***." Teaching the
trigger — *no "and" → nothing to change, that's a clean pass* — is what made it stick, and she
then ran it correctly herself. ➡️ **For any future rule, say when it does NOT apply, in the
same breath.** She is a rule-applier; give her the boundary or she'll apply it everywhere.

**2. She thinks verbs pluralize like nouns.** She answered *"Sophie and her friends **knows**"*
— she knew it was a group and *added* the -s. Nouns take -s for plural; verbs take -s for
**singular**. That flip is not obvious and had never been said to her. Worth repeating once.

**3. Her limit on a single correction is about two rounds.** After two nudges toward
*"Dumbledore take risk"* she wrote *"cna i just do ace"* — typo and all, which is the tell.
That was not laziness; it was the ninth minute on one verb. ➡️ **Two guesses, then hand her
the answer and move on.** The correction is worth less than her staying in the session.

**4. She now argues the other side unprompted.** Nobody asked for a counterargument and her
closing line handled the strongest objection to her own claim: *"even though Voldemort wasn't
dangerous back then, Dumbledore knew he would be dangerous one day."* On 07-14 this was a
Tier-3 stretch she had to be handed a frame for. Six weeks later it's just how she ends a
paragraph. **Stop offering it as a stretch — start expecting it and praising it by name.**

## Talk to her like this

✅ *"Read that sentence out loud — you'll hear it."* (this is her superpower, name it)
✅ *"You noticed which NAME he uses. That's what real readers do."*
✅ *"Try this exact sentence: In the book, ______ said, '______.'"* (frames get done)

❌ *"Check your verbs."* (silent checking finds her nothing — say **read aloud** instead)
❌ *"Add a few sentences about why it mattered."* (too open — she'll decline)
❌ Correcting her honesty or her jokes into something more polite
