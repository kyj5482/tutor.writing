# Grade 6 Examples — *Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief* by Rick Riordan

All examples below imagine a 6th grader who read **chapters 1–5**: Percy Jackson, 12, has
dyslexia and ADHD and has been kicked out of six schools. On a field trip to the
Metropolitan Museum of Art, his pre-algebra teacher Mrs. Dodds turns into a winged monster
and attacks him — and his Latin teacher, Mr. Brunner, tosses him a pen that becomes a
sword. Afterward, nobody remembers Mrs. Dodds ever existing. Percy goes home to his mom
Sally and his awful stepfather, Gabe. On a trip to Montauk, his best friend Grover shows up
in a panic — Grover is a satyr — and a Minotaur attacks them in a storm. Percy's mother
vanishes in a flash of golden light, and Percy kills the Minotaur with its own horn and
stumbles into Camp Half-Blood.

Examples are written at **Tier 2** unless marked. They're good, not perfect — that's the
point. Your writing should sound like YOU.

**👀 Notice in every example:** the quote is in the **main writing**, not saved for the
bonus round. Look for the "quotation marks" each time.

---

## 01 — Summary

Today I read the first five chapters of *Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief*, where
Percy finds out his whole life has been a lie. First, on a museum field trip, his math
teacher Mrs. Dodds turns into a monster and attacks him, and Mr. Brunner throws him a pen
that turns into a sword. After that, the weirdest part happens — nobody remembers Mrs.
Dodds at all, and when Percy asks about her, his friend Grover just says, "Who?" Then
Percy goes to Montauk with his mom, and Grover shows up saying something is coming after
them. Finally, a Minotaur attacks their car in a storm, Percy's mom disappears in a flash
of gold light, and Percy kills the monster with its own horn. Now Percy is at a camp for
kids like him, and he still doesn't know if his mother is dead.

## 02 — Prediction

I predict that Percy's real dad is one of the Greek gods, and that's why monsters keep
finding him. One clue is that Percy has never met his father, and his mom gets sad and
mysterious every time he asks. Another clue is that Grover turned out to be a satyr who was
sent to protect him — you don't send a bodyguard for a normal kid. My confidence is 9 out of
10, because the camp is literally called Camp Half-Blood, and "half" has to mean half god.

## 03 — Character Diary

Dear Diary,
Today I found out I'm not crazy, which is somehow worse.
I killed my math teacher. She had wings. And then everyone looked at me like I was making it
up, and after a while I started wondering if maybe I was. That's the part that got me — not
the monster, the forgetting. Then Grover shows up at Montauk out of nowhere with no pants on
(long story) and starts talking about my scent, like I'm a sandwich.
And then Mom. There was a flash of gold and she was just *gone*, like she was never standing
there at all. I'm not writing about that part yet.
I'm at a camp now. My arms hurt. I have a monster horn and I don't remember picking it up.
— Percy

## 04 — Opinion / Response

I think Percy's mom was right to keep the truth from him, even though it made his life
harder. My evidence is that when Percy asks about his father, she tells him, "I can't,
Percy. I'm sorry." She isn't being mean — she is protecting him, because the second Percy
learns who he is, the monsters can find him. This shows that some secrets in this book
aren't lies, they're shields. That's why I think she made the right choice, even though
Percy spent twelve years feeling like something was wrong with him.

## 05 — Letter to a Character

Dear Percy,
When everyone pretended Mrs. Dodds never existed, I got so frustrated for you. That would
make anyone feel insane. You should trust Grover more than you do right now — I know he's
been weird and he lied to you, but he's been trying to keep you alive this whole time.
I have two questions: Did you ever wonder why you're always the one who gets in trouble
and never the kid who started it? And do you actually believe your mom is gone?
Also, be careful at that camp. I know something you don't. Not everyone there is going to
be on your side.
Your friend, Mina

## 06 — Rewrite the Scene (What If…?)

In the book, Mr. Brunner covers for Percy after the Mrs. Dodds fight and pretends nothing
happened. But what if he had told Percy the truth right there in the museum?

Mr. Brunner rolled his wheelchair between Percy and the doorway. For a second he didn't
look like a teacher at all.
"Sit down, Percy."
"I'm not crazy," Percy said. "She had wings. She had actual —"
"I know exactly what she had." Mr. Brunner's voice was very quiet. "That is the problem."
Percy stared at him. "Then why is everyone acting like —"
"Because the Mist is kind to them." Mr. Brunner handed him back the pen. "It is not going
to be kind to you. Keep this in your pocket, and stop asking questions in hallways."
Down in the lobby, the fountain water leaned toward Percy as he walked past. He didn't
notice. Mr. Brunner did.

## 07 — Golden Line

**My golden line:** "Look, I didn't want to be a half-blood." (chapter 1, the very first
sentence)

In the story, this is Percy talking straight to the reader before anything even happens. I
think the author wrote it this way because it sounds like Percy is already tired, like he's
warning us instead of bragging. Most hero books start with someone excited to be special,
but this one starts with someone who wishes he wasn't. The word "Look" is what makes it —
it's the way you talk to someone who won't believe you. It makes me wonder what happened to
him that was bad enough to make him start the story like that.

## 08 — Connection

In the book, Percy has been kicked out of six schools and everyone treats him like he's the
problem, even when he isn't the one who started it. This reminds me of a kid in my class who
got blamed every time our table got loud, because he had gotten in trouble before. They're
alike because once people decide who you are, they stop actually looking at you. But they're
different too, because Percy actually *does* do the things — he really did shoot his friend
with a water cannon — he just can't explain why weird stuff follows him around. I understand
Percy better now, because being blamed for something you can't explain is worse than being
blamed for something you did.

## 09 — Quick Write 3-2-1 *(for a short-reading day)*

**3 things that happened:**
1. Mrs. Dodds turned into a winged monster on the museum field trip.
2. Mr. Brunner threw Percy a pen that turned into a sword.
3. Nobody remembered Mrs. Dodds afterward — even the school records were changed.

**2 words:** "Kindly ones" — Grover says it once and then looks scared, so I think it's a
polite name for something not kind at all, like people are afraid to say the real name.
"Mist" — I think it means something that makes normal people see normal things instead of
monsters.

**1 question:** If the Mist makes everyone forget, why doesn't it work on Percy? My guess is
that whatever makes Percy different is the same thing the monsters can smell.

## 10 — Book Review *(written as if the whole book was finished)*

What if the reason you're bad at school is that your brain was built for something else
entirely? *Percy Jackson and the Lightning Thief* by Rick Riordan is about a twelve-year-old
who gets blamed for stealing Zeus's master lightning bolt and has ten days to cross the
country and return it.

The best thing about this book is how it turns Percy's dyslexia and ADHD into the proof
that he's a demigod — his brain is wired for Ancient Greek and for battle, not for
worksheets. That flips something real kids feel bad about into the best part of them. The
chapters end on cliffhangers constantly, so it's very hard to stop.

One thing that could be better is that the middle has a lot of monster fights in a row, and
after a while they blur together. I give it 4.5 out of 5 stars, losing half a star for that
middle stretch but earning the rest because the ending twist made me go back and reread
chapter one. If you liked Harry Potter, you'll love this — it has the same "kid finds out
he belongs to a hidden world" feeling, but it's funnier and faster.

## 11 — ACE Write (Bonus) *(tutor asked: "Why does it matter so much that nobody remembers Mrs. Dodds?")*

**A —** It matters because it means Percy can't trust his own memory, and that's scarier
than the monster was.

**C —** In the book, after the fight, Percy asks Grover about Mrs. Dodds and Grover just
says, "Who?" Even the school paperwork says Mrs. Kerr was always the math teacher.

**E —** This shows that Percy is completely alone with what he saw. A monster you can fight,
but you can't fight everybody telling you it didn't happen. That's why Percy starts thinking
maybe he really is the problem, which is exactly what the adults around him have let him
believe his whole life.
