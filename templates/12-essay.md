# 12 — Essay

**Skill:** holding one idea across several paragraphs
**Best when:** you've read a good chunk, or just finished a book
**Ladder stage:** 🏛️ Essay Writer (stage 4)

---

## 🧱 Read this first — you do NOT write this in one day

An essay is not a longer journal entry. It's a few short pieces stacked in order.
So build it the way you'd build anything big: **one piece a day.**

| Day | What you write | About how long |
|-----|----------------|----------------|
| **Day 1** | Your thesis + your first evidence paragraph | a normal entry |
| **Day 2** | Your second evidence paragraph | a normal entry |
| **Day 3** | The ending + read the whole thing and fix it | a normal entry |

Each day is a **normal-sized session** — the same amount you already write. Nothing gets
harder; the pieces just add up. On Day 3 you paste the whole thing together, and you have
written a real essay.

You *can* write it in one sitting if you're on a roll. You just never have to.

**Every day still counts:** each day is its own journal entry, its own XP, and your streak
keeps going. Tell your tutor `Essay day 2 of 3` and they'll set it up.

---

## The shape (this is the whole trick)

```
THESIS        — one sentence. What do you claim about this book?
   ↓
BECAUSE #1    — reason + quote + why that quote proves it
   ↓
BECAUSE #2    — reason + quote + why that quote proves it
   ↓
SO WHAT       — an ending that says something NEW, not a repeat
```

The only new thing here is the **thesis** — one sentence that someone could disagree with.
"*Holes* is a good book" is not a thesis; nobody disagrees. "*Holes* is really about a debt,
not a crime" is a thesis.

---

## Tier 1 — the frame (3 paragraphs, ~150 words)

Fill in the blanks. Press Enter twice between each paragraph.

**Paragraph 1**
- In ______ (book), I think ______.
- I think this because of two things.

**Paragraph 2**
- The first reason is ______.
- In the book, ______ (what happened, or a short quote).
- This shows ______.

**Paragraph 3**
- The second reason is ______.
- In the book, ______.
- This is why I think ______.

✅ Checklist: **three paragraphs with blank lines between them** · a claim someone could
argue with · one detail from the book in each middle paragraph.

---

## Tier 2 — a real essay (4 paragraphs, ~250 words)

- **Open with the thesis**, not with "I am going to write about." Just say the claim.
- **Two body paragraphs.** Each one: a reason → a short **quote** in "quotation marks" →
  a sentence explaining *why that quote proves the reason*.
- **Close with a "so what?"** — something you now understand that you didn't say at the
  start. Never just repeat your thesis in different words.
- Link your paragraphs: *One reason… · The bigger reason… · What this means is…*

✅ Checklist: thesis in the first two sentences · 2 quotes, one per body paragraph ·
each quote followed by a "this shows / this matters because" · an ending that ADDS.

---

## Tier 3 — an essay with a spine (4–5 paragraphs, 350+ words)

Everything in Tier 2, plus:

- **Put the strongest reason last**, not first. Build toward it.
- **Fold in the other side** *inside* your own argument, in one or two sentences:
  *"Some might say ______, and that's fair — but ______."* (If you want a whole paragraph
  of this, that's template 13.)
- **Embed your quotes** inside your own sentences instead of dropping them in:
  - Dropped: *He is scared. "His hands were shaking."*
  - Embedded: *Sachar never says Stanley is scared — he just lets us watch "his hands
    shaking" over a shovel he can barely lift.*
- **Vary your sentence lengths.** A short one after two long ones hits hard.
- Your ending should be the best sentence in the piece. Write it last, on purpose.

✅ Checklist: strongest reason last · a concession + rebuttal · embedded quotes ·
a closing insight you did not state at the beginning.

---

## If you get stuck

| Stuck on | Try this |
|----------|----------|
| The thesis | Finish this out loud: *"What people miss about this book is ______."* |
| A body paragraph | You already have the reason. Go find the sentence in the book that proves it, and copy it exactly. |
| The ending | Ask: *"What do I understand now that I didn't on page 1?"* Write that. |
| It feels too long | You're trying to do it in one day. Do Day 1 only and stop. |
