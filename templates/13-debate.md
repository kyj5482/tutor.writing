# 13 — Debate

**Skill:** arguing the other side better than they can, then answering it
**Best when:** something in the book was genuinely arguable — a choice, a rule, a betrayal
**Ladder stage:** ⚖️ Debater (stage 5)

---

## The one idea behind this template

Most people argue like this: *here's my side, and the other side is dumb.*

That's the weak version, and everybody can tell. The strong version is:

> **Make the other side sound RIGHT. Then explain why you still disagree.**

If your opponent reads your version of their argument and thinks *"yes, that's exactly what
I meant"* — now your answer actually counts for something. A debate you win against a weak
version of the other side isn't a win.

This is the single most grown-up move in writing, and it is the thing university essays are
mostly made of.

---

## The shape

```
THE QUESTION       — state it as a real question with two possible answers
   ↓
MY ANSWER          — pick a side. Actually pick one.
   ↓
THE BEST CASE      — the strongest version of the OTHER side, said fairly
AGAINST ME           (with evidence from the book — not a strawman)
   ↓
MY ANSWER TO IT    — "That's true, but…" — what the other side can't explain
   ↓
WHERE I LAND       — your side, now stronger because it survived a real test
```

---

## Tier 1 — the frame (~150 words)

- The question is: should ______ have ______?
- I think ______.
- **Some people would say ______.** That makes sense because ______.
- **But I still think ______, because ______.**

✅ Checklist: a real question · you pick a side · you say the other side **fairly** (no
"but that's silly") · one "but" sentence that answers it.

---

## Tier 2 — a fair fight (~250 words, 4 paragraphs)

- **Paragraph 1** — the question, and your answer in one clear sentence.
- **Paragraph 2** — your reason, with a **quote** from the book.
- **Paragraph 3** — *"The strongest argument against me is ______."* Give it a **real
  reason and its own quote**. Make it good. This paragraph should be genuinely convincing.
- **Paragraph 4** — *"That's true. But ______."* Say what the other side leaves out,
  then land your side.

**The words that do the work:**
- Opening the other side: *Some might argue… · It's fair to say… · The strongest case
  against me is…*
- Conceding honestly: *That's true. · I understand the objection. · They're right that…*
- Turning it: *But that misses… · Even so… · What that can't explain is…*

✅ Checklist: the other side gets its **own quote** · you actually concede something true ·
your "but" names what the other side can't explain.

---

## Tier 3 — a debate you could lose (350+ words)

Everything in Tier 2, plus:

- **Concede something that actually costs you.** Not a fake concession
  ("some people don't like this book, but they're wrong"). A real one: *"choosing freely
  is what makes a sacrifice mean something, and Katniss doesn't choose — I understand the
  objection."*
- **Answer with a reframe, not a repeat.** Don't restate your reason louder. Show that the
  other side is answering a *different question* than the one that matters.
- **Name what would change your mind.** One sentence: *"If ______ were true, I'd switch
  sides."* This is what real thinkers do, and almost nobody your age does it.
- **Don't be smug at the end.** The strongest closing sounds like someone who thought hard,
  not someone who won.

✅ Checklist: a concession that costs you · a reframe instead of a repeat · one sentence
naming what would change your mind · no gloating.

---

## Picking a question worth arguing

A good debate question has a real *yes* and a real *no*. Test it: can you argue the other
side for 30 seconds without laughing? If not, pick a different question.

| ❌ Not arguable | ✅ Arguable |
|---|---|
| Was Voldemort bad? | Was Dumbledore right to keep Harry in the dark? |
| Is Camp Green Lake unfair? | Did digging actually change Stanley — or did Zero? |
| Was Keefe wrong to betray them? | Could Keefe's plan have worked if he'd told Sophie? |

**Try it out loud first.** Argue the side you *don't* believe for 30 seconds. If you can do
that, you're ready to write.
