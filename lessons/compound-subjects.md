# Mini-lesson — Two doers joined by "and"

**For:** Jaei (7 slips since 06-23) **and** Jia (07-24) — same error, same cause.
**Time:** 3 minutes. **Reward:** +5 XP.
**Run it when:** the Watch item has been 0/3 for three sessions. That's now.

---

## Step 1 — Say this first (the honest opener)

> "This one keeps coming back, and that's on me — I've been handing you the fixed sentence
> instead of the rule. Here's the actual rule. Two minutes and it's yours for good."

Do not skip this. Seven corrections with no explanation is the tutor's error, not the
student's, and saying so out loud is what makes the lesson land instead of stinging.

## Step 2 — Show them their own sentences

**Jaei — you wrote all of these:**

| | Your sentence | The verb matched… |
|---|---|---|
| 06-23 | *"Teresa and Aris **was** forced to act"* | Aris |
| 07-07 | *"me and my sister **was** out of here"* | my sister |
| 07-14 | *"trust and loyalty **is** the theme"* | loyalty |
| 07-23 | *"the plot and the action **is** just excellent"* | the action |
| 07-25 | *"Amy and Dan **was** watching"* | Dan |

**Jia — you wrote this one:**

> *"Snape and Malfoy **is** a Death Eater"* (07-24) → the verb matched **Malfoy**

Now ask them: **"What do all of these have in common?"** Let them find it. They will.

Then name it:

> **You matched the verb to the word standing closest to it — not to the whole subject.**

This is worth saying plainly: *that is a smart-sounding mistake.* Your ear picks the nearest
noun because that's the last thing it heard. Every one of these sentences **sounds fine out
loud** — which is exactly why reading aloud never caught them, and why this needed a rule
instead of another correction.

## Step 3 — The rule (one sentence)

> ### When **and** joins two doers, they become a group. A group is plural.

That's it. Not "sometimes." Not "usually." Always.

- Amy is one. Dan is one. **Amy and Dan** = a group = **were**.
- Snape is one. Malfoy is one. **Snape and Malfoy** = a group = **are** (and *Death Eater**s***).
- The plot is one. The action is one. **The plot and the action** = a group = **are**.

**The trap to point out:** the noun sitting right before the verb is often singular
(*Dan*, *Malfoy*, *the action*). Ignore it. Look at the **whole** subject.

## Step 4 — Practice out loud (3 items, 30 seconds)

Don't write these down. Just say them.

1. Harry and Ron ____ (is / are) in the same house.
2. The hatchet and the fire ____ (was / were) all Brian had.
3. Sophie and her friends ____ (knows / know) the way to the hideout.

*(are · were · know)*

Miss one? No problem — point back at the rule and try it again. Nobody moves on annoyed.

**Bonus round if they're enjoying it** — the sneaky one:

4. The box of clues ____ (was / were) hidden.

*(**was** — "of clues" isn't a doer. **One box.** This is the mirror image of their error,
and getting it right proves they've got the actual rule and not just "and means add an s.")*

## Step 5 — The trick they keep

### ✌️ The Two-Finger Check

> Before you save: find every **and** in a subject. Hold up **two fingers**.
> Two fingers = **are / were / know** (no -s on the verb).

Three seconds, and it's physical — which is the point. It works where reading aloud fails,
because you're **looking** for the word *and*, not listening for a sound.

**Say it their way:**
- **Jaei** (wants the reason): *"Your ear grabs the nearest noun. The two-finger check
  overrules your ear — that's why it works."*
- **Jia** (works by sound): *"This is the one your ear can't catch, so use your fingers
  instead. Everything else, keep reading aloud."*

## Step 6 — After the lesson

- Award **+5 XP**, log it in the Session Log as `lesson: compound subjects`.
- Reset the Watch item's clean streak to **0/3** and note *"rule taught <date>"*.
- For the next 3 sessions, if it slips: **do not re-explain.** Say *"two-finger check"* and
  let them find it. If they fix it themselves, that still counts as a clean entry — the
  goal is the habit, not a perfect first draft.
- If it's clean for 3 sessions → retire the item and **celebrate it loudly**. Ending a
  two-month mistake is a bigger win than any badge.

## 부모님께

재이는 이 실수를 **7번** 지적받았지만 고쳐지지 않았습니다. 이유를 찾았어요: 재이는 동사를
**바로 앞에 있는 명사**에 맞추고 있었습니다 ("Amy and Dan **was**" → Dan에 맞춤). 지아도
**똑같은 실수**를 같은 이유로 했습니다 ("Snape and Malfoy **is**" → Malfoy에 맞춤).

지금까지는 고친 문장만 알려줬을 뿐 **규칙을 설명한 적이 없었습니다.** 이건 아이들의 부주의가
아니라 가르치는 방식의 문제였어요.

또 하나 중요한 점: 이 실수는 **소리 내어 읽어도 안 잡힙니다.** "Amy and Dan was"는 규칙을
모르면 자연스럽게 들리거든요. 그래서 소리가 아닌 **손가락 두 개 확인법**(주어에 'and'가 있으면
손가락 두 개 → 복수 동사)을 알려줬습니다. 집에서 글을 볼 기회가 있으면 **"and 있어? 손가락 둘?"**
한 마디만 해주시면 됩니다.
