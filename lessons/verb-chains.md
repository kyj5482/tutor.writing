# Mini-lesson — Verb chains: only ONE verb carries the tense

**For:** Jaei. **Time:** 3 minutes. **Reward:** +5 XP.
**Run it when:** the *"could have wrote"* Watch item is still at 0/3 after being named
before the writing. Ran **2026-08-01**.

> ⚠️ **Do not send the read-aloud after this error.** It is inaudible — *"could have wrote"*
> sounds completely fine, and on 07-31 his voice fixed *"many conflict" → "conflicts"* in the
> same pass that walked straight over *"could have wrote"*. His ear is reliable for sound-shaped
> errors and useless for rule-shaped ones. This is rule-shaped. It gets the rule.

---

## Step 1 — Take the blame, out loud

> "You've had this pointed at twice and it's still there, so the problem isn't you — it's that
> I kept handing you the fixed sentence instead of the rule. Same mistake I made with compound
> subjects for seven weeks. Here's the rule. You'll have it for good in three minutes."

This is true and he has the receipts: compound subjects took **7 corrections = 0 progress**, then
**1 rule = cleared in 3 sessions.** Say the parallel plainly. He respects the accounting.

## Step 2 — The pattern, in his own sentences

| Date | What he wrote | The word in front |
|------|---------------|-------------------|
| 07-07 | *"they could **have ran** away"* | have |
| 07-31 | *"could **have wrote** other story"* | have |
| 08-01 | *"could **have wrote** another story for this book"* | have |
| 08-01 | *"This frustration **helps built** the concentration"* | helps |

Ask him what the first three have in common **before** telling him. He diagnosed the
compound-subject pattern on his first guess; give him the chance to do it again.

Then point at the fourth one, because it's the interesting one — it breaks the other way.

## Step 3 — The rule (one line)

> ### In a chain of verbs, only the FIRST one carries the tense. Every verb after it takes the form the word in front of it demands.

*Wrote* is a **tense**. But in *"could have ___"*, the tense has already been used up by *could*.
So the last verb isn't allowed to carry one — it has to switch to the form that carries none.

## Step 4 — Which form, decided by one word

Every verb has three forms. These are the ones he actually uses:

| 1st (base) | 2nd (past) | 3rd (participle) |
|---|---|---|
| write | wrote | **written** |
| run | ran | **run** |
| see | saw | **seen** |
| take | took | **taken** |
| build | built | **built** |
| go | went | **gone** |

And the rule for picking:

> **after have / has / had → 3rd form**
> *could have **written*** · *has **taken*** · *had **gone***
>
> **after can · could · will · would · should · must · to · help · make · let → 1st form**
> *could **write*** · *helps **build*** · *made her **meet*** · *to **finish***

That's the whole thing. Two rows. **His two errors are one row each**, which is why they look
different and are actually the same mistake: he lets the second verb try to carry a tense.

## Step 5 — Practice out loud (20 seconds)

1. Messenger could have ____ a different book six. *(wrote / written)*
2. The frustration helps ____ the concentration. *(built / build)*
3. The Neverseen has ____ Sophie's parents. *(took / taken)*

*(written · build · taken)*

Item 2 is his own sentence from today. Item 3 is his own book. Use those, not invented ones.

## Step 6 — The trick he keeps

### 👉 Look at the word in front

> Before you write a verb, look at the word **immediately before it**.
> **have / has / had** → 3rd form (*written, taken, gone*)
> **anything else** — could, will, to, help, make, let → 1st form (*write, take, go*)

One word. One look. It's a check, not a feeling — which is the kind he can actually run.

## Step 7 — After the lesson

- Award **+5 XP**, log as `lesson: verb chains`.
- **Never re-explain this rule.** If it slips, say four words: **"word in front."** Nothing else.
- Keep the Watch item, but rewrite it to point at the trick rather than the error.
- Expect it to clear fast. Compound subjects went 0/3 → cleared in three sessions once he had
  the rule, and this one is simpler.

## 부모님께

재이가 *"could have wrote"*(→ written), *"helps built"*(→ build) 같은 실수를 반복했는데,
**지금까지 규칙을 설명해 준 적이 한 번도 없었습니다** — 고친 문장만 알려줬어요.

규칙은 하나입니다: **동사가 연달아 나올 때 시제는 첫 번째 동사만 담당합니다.** 그래서
*have/has/had* 뒤에는 3형(written, taken), *could/will/to/help* 뒤에는 1형(write, take)이
옵니다.

이 실수는 **소리 내어 읽어도 잡히지 않습니다** — "could have wrote"는 귀에 자연스럽게
들리거든요. 그래서 재이에게는 "귀"가 아니라 "규칙"으로 접근했습니다. 실제로 지난번 주어-동사
일치 문제도 일곱 번 고쳐줬을 때는 전혀 나아지지 않다가, 규칙을 한 번 가르치자 세 번 만에
해결됐습니다.
