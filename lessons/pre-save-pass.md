# The pre-save pass — why silent proofreading can't work

**Who:** Jaei (2026-08-20) · **Time:** ~3 min · **+5 XP**

## The evidence — his own sentences

Four slips that reached the saved file on 2026-08-17:

> a **moments** hesitation · **hastly** · **Your** doing what now? · **lets** go save the alicorn

**Ask first:** *"What do all four have in common?"*

The answer is not "carelessness" and it is not "you don't know these." He fixes every one of
them in two seconds when pointed at. They are not knowledge gaps — **they are sentences nobody
read.**

## The rule, once

> **You can't proofread silently, because your eye reads what you *meant*. Your mouth reads
> what's actually there.**

This is why "check it again before you save" had failed for two months. Re-reading silently
re-runs the sentence you intended to write. The eye supplies the missing apostrophe, the missing
letter in *hastily*, the *you're* you meant. Nothing is wrong with his attention — the method
was wrong.

## The check (hand him this, then stop explaining)

👉 **20 seconds. Out loud. Before saving. And he starts it, not the tutor.**

Anything his mouth trips on is the slip. He does not need to know *where* it is in advance —
finding it is the whole exercise.

## Afterwards

Point at the **check**, never at the error: *"Run the check."* Telling him which sentence is
wrong teaches him that sentence and nothing else — the exact failure that made the compound-
subject error survive seven corrections.

**Clean = he ran it without being asked.** Being reminded and complying is not the skill; the
skill is starting it himself.
