# Mini-lessons — for mistakes that won't go away

## Why this folder exists

The Watch list is good at *spotting* a repeated mistake. It turned out to be bad at
*ending* one.

Two months of evidence, 35 entries:

- Jaei was handed the corrected sentence for compound subjects **7 times** (06-23, 07-04,
  07-07, 07-14, 07-16, 07-23, 07-25). His clean streak never got above 0/3.
- Jia's verb slips appear in her **first entry** (06-09) and her **most recent** one (07-24).

Both kids fix the error instantly, every time, the moment it's pointed out. So this was
never about effort or attention. **Nobody had ever taught them the rule** — they were only
ever shown the repaired sentence, which teaches you that one sentence and nothing else.

**The trigger:** when a Watch-list item sits at **0/3 for three sessions**, stop correcting
it. Teach it once, properly, with a lesson from this folder. Then go back to the Watch list.

## How to run one (about 3 minutes, inside a normal session)

1. **Name it, kindly.** "This one keeps coming back — that's my fault, not yours. I've been
   giving you the fix instead of the rule. Two minutes and it's yours forever."
2. **Show the pattern in THEIR OWN sentences.** Every lesson is built from real sentences
   they actually wrote. It is much more convincing than invented examples.
3. **One rule, one sentence long.** If the rule needs a paragraph, it's the wrong rule.
4. **Three practice items**, out loud, right there. Do not save these to the journal —
   this is practice, not a graded entry.
5. **Give them the trick** — the 3-second physical check they run before saving.
6. Award **+5 XP** for completing a mini-lesson. It's real work.

Then, in the next 3 sessions, the 🔧 improvement slot goes to something else. If the
mistake reappears, point at the **trick**, not at the error: *"Run the check."*

## Match the method to the kid

This matters more than the lesson content:

| | Jia | Jaei |
|---|---|---|
| **What works** | her **ear** — she self-corrects on every read-aloud | his **logic** — he wants the rule and the reason |
| **What fails** | silent proofreading (catches her nothing) | being handed the fix again (7 tries, 0 for 7) |
| **So teach with** | say it out loud, listen for it | name the rule, show why the error is tempting |

Note that "read it aloud" **cannot** fix a compound-subject error — *"Snape and Malfoy is"*
sounds perfectly fine if you don't know the rule. That's exactly why that one needed a
lesson and the spelling item didn't.

## The lessons

| File | Rule | Who | Why now |
|------|------|-----|---------|
| `compound-subjects.md` | two doers joined by **and** take a plural verb | **both** | Jaei 7 slips; Jia 1 slip, same cause |
| `verb-endings.md` | -ed / -s endings you can hear | Jia | in her 1st and her 18th entry |
| `a-vs-an.md` | **an** before a vowel sound | both | 5 slips between them, never once addressed |
| `paragraph-breaks.md` | one idea, one paragraph | Jia (queued) | every long entry is a single block |

Queued lessons wait their turn — never run two in one session, and never let a lesson
replace the day's writing.
